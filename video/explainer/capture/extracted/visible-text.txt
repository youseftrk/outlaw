Qalaa in one line: "One switch that grants — and instantly takes back — an AI agent's power."

The UAE is building government and enterprise on AI agents, acting on real systems and real data. The question nobody has answered: when an agent from one organisation needs to touch another organisation's data or systems — say, during an emergency — who says yes, for how long, and who can say stop?

Verified claims only (research: docs/research/qalaa-research.md on branch devin/1790342274-qalaa-authority-pivot):
- "In April 2026 the UAE set a two-year goal: half of all government services and operations run on AI agents." (mediaoffice.ae, 23 Apr 2026)

- "Dubai put a Chief AI Officer in every government department — more than 230 so far."
- Gartner: "over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by 2027 — inadequate risk controls is a named reason."
The phrase "thousands of AI agents" could NOT be verified — do not say it.

Qalaa is the answer:
1. Ask. The agent asks for a specific permission: what, where, why, for how long.
2. The owner says yes. Only the organisation that owns the data can grant it. Nobody grants themselves power. For sensitive things, a human confirms with a one-time code.
3. It works — for exactly that. The agent's request now goes through. Nothing more, nowhere else, no longer than agreed.
4. Stop. The owner flips the switch. The agent's very next request is refused. Not "soon" — now.
5. Everything is written down. Every ask, every yes, every action, every stop — in a record anyone can read. The data never leaves the organisation that owns it.

Who it serves: governments and large enterprises that run many AI agents across departments — UAE first (sovereignty: the data and the decision stay with the UAE entity; the switch is yours, not a vendor's).
